Sensitivity: Adjusted CYP and Recorded Births

DHIS2
births
contraception
CYP
sensitivity
Redistributing long-acting contraceptive protection across calendar years and comparing it with recorded births.
Published

September 21, 2026

1 Measures compared

The old measure is the standard CYP presented in the main DHIS report. It credits all expected protection from a long-acting method in its insertion year. The new measure spreads implant and IUCD protection across the calendar years in which it is expected to remain active and applies the proposed pill and implant factors.

A bridge measure, timing only, redistributes implant and IUCD protection while preserving every old standard factor, isolating the timing change from the additional factor changes in the full proposal.

Important

This is an exploratory ecological comparison of two national annual series. A correlation does not estimate contraceptive effectiveness or establish that contraception caused the observed birth trend. Both series may be affected by reporting completeness, method mix, population size and unmeasured changes over time.

Recorded births are defined as live births in facilities plus births born alive before arrival. Stillbirths are excluded. The standard CYP series is unchanged from the main report.

2 Allocation assumptions

The adjusted measure applies each year’s method-specific weight to services recorded in year \(N-k\), where \(k\) is the protection-year offset. Thus, adjusted protection in calendar year \(N\) is

\[ \operatorname{Adjusted\ CYP}_N = \sum_m \sum_k \operatorname{Services}_{m,N-k} w_{m,k}. \]

The full proposal uses an implant profile of \(1.00, 0.80, 0.70\), totalling 2.5 CYP. For the timing-only bridge, the same relative profile is scaled to total 3.5 CYP so that only timing changes. The working IUCD profile starts at 0.85 and 0.75 and declines over ten years, totalling 4.6 CYP in both new measures. Pills use 12 cycles per CYP in the full proposal and the old 15-cycle factor in the timing-only bridge. These are scenario assumptions, not locally estimated continuation rates.

Warning

The full old-versus-new difference combines a timing change with two factor changes: pill credit rises by 25% per cycle and total implant credit falls by 28.6% per insertion. It must not be interpreted as the effect of continuation allocation alone. The timing-only bridge provides that separate comparison.

3 Pre-2015 cohorts

The DHIS series begins in 2015. Consequently, active implant and IUCD cohorts inserted before 2015 cannot be observed. Two versions of the full proposal show the effect of this boundary:

  • Observed cohorts only: uses only insertions observed from 2015 onward and is therefore left-truncated in early years.
  • Steady-state initialization: assumes the 2015 method-specific count also occurred in each required pre-2015 year. This is a transparent warm-up assumption, not an estimate of historical insertions.

The steady-state version is used for the main old-versus-new comparison. The observed-cohort version is retained only as a boundary sensitivity check.

4 Differences from old standard CYP

The timing-only columns isolate the change from moving protection across calendar years. The full-proposal columns add the changed pill and implant factors.

5 Alignment with recorded births

Two temporal alignments are evaluated with Pearson’s correlation coefficient:

  • Previous-year births: CYP in year \(N\) versus births in year \(N-1\), the relationship motivating this sensitivity analysis.
  • Following-year births: CYP in year \(N\) versus births in year \(N+1\), the hypothesised forecasting relationship.

Each estimate uses ten annual pairs. No p-values are reported because the short, serially ordered time series does not satisfy the independence assumption of a simple correlation test.

6 Correlation with births: old versus new

Births in the following year: old standard CYP \(r = -0.502\), timing only \(r = -0.440\), full proposal \(r = -0.353\).

Births in the previous year: old standard CYP \(r = -0.791\), timing only \(r = -0.755\), full proposal \(r = -0.709\).
