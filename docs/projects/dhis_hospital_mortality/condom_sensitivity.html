How much does total national CYP change if we assume distributed condoms are not all actually used?
Published

August 20, 2026

1 Why this check?

Couple-years of protection (CYP) treats every distributed condom as if it were used correctly for its full protective purpose. Two things make that assumption shakier for condoms than for clinic-administered methods (injectables, implants, IUCD, sterilisation): the DHIS2 figure is units distributed to facilities, not confirmed use, and there is no data here on whether distributed condoms were used correctly (or at all).

This page holds the standard USAID/FP2030 factor (120 units = 1 CYP) fixed and instead varies assumed real-world utilization — the share of distributed condoms assumed actually used — to show how sensitive total national CYP is to that assumption.

Important

The primary report (DHIS2 Hospital Indicators) always uses the “Standard (100% used, current default)” scenario below. This page is a supplementary robustness check — it does not change any number reported elsewhere.
