Continuation-weighted allocation of couple-years of protection

Methods

1 Purpose

Standard couple-years of protection (CYP) credits all the protection a commodity provides to the year it is distributed (Data for Impact (D4I), n.d.). For short-acting methods this is a reasonable approximation. For long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARCs), it front-loads several years of protection into a single year. A 10-year copper IUD, for example, contributes 4.6 CYP to its insertion year.

Birth forecasting needs protection in the years it is actually in effect. We therefore allocate each year’s distribution across its protection timeline using method-specific continuation weights. Total CYP over a cohort’s lifetime is unchanged; only its timing differs.

2 Notation

\(D_{m,s}\) should be units issued to clients (insertions for implants and IUDs), not deliveries to facilities. Facility stock that is never inserted provides no protection.

3 Standard CYP

\[ \text{CYP}^{\text{std}}_{m,t} = c_m \, D_{m,t} \tag{1}\]

4 Continuation-weighted CYP

Protection in year \(t\) is the sum of contributions from every distribution cohort still within its protection window:

\[ P_{m,t} = \sum_{k=0}^{L_m - 1} w_{m,k} \, D_{m,t-k} \tag{2}\]

\[ P_t = \sum_m P_{m,t} \tag{3}\]

The weights satisfy three conditions:

\[ \sum_{k=0}^{L_m - 1} w_{m,k} = c_m \tag{4}\]

\[ 0 \le w_{m,k} \le 1 \tag{5}\]

\[ w_{m,k+1} \le w_{m,k} \tag{6}\]

Equation 4 keeps the lifetime CYP of each cohort equal to standard reporting, so the two approaches can be reconciled. Equation 5 holds because one unit cannot protect more than one couple for one year. Equation 6 reflects that discontinuation only removes users over time; it applies to years of use, not to the calendar-year weights in Equation 8.

In matrix form, for years \(t_0, \dots, T\), \(\mathbf{P}_m = \mathbf{W}_m \mathbf{D}_m\), where \(\mathbf{W}_m\) is a lower-triangular band matrix with \(w_{m,0}\) on the diagonal, \(w_{m,1}\) on the first sub-diagonal, and so on up to \(w_{m,L_m-1}\).

4.1 Relationship to geometric decay

A single annual decay rate \(\delta_m\) is a special case of Equation 2:

\[ w_{m,k} = w_{m,0} (1 - \delta_m)^k, \qquad k = 0, \dots, L_m - 1 \tag{7}\]

with \(w_{m,0}\) chosen so that Equation 4 holds. The empirical schedules below are not geometric (the implant’s year-on-year ratios are 0.80 and 0.875), so we use the weights directly.

5 Weight schedules

Conversion factors follow the USAID-endorsed values (Data for Impact (D4I), n.d.; Lebetkin et al. 2024).

The combined-pill factor assumes 28-pill cycles. The progestin-only factor of 12 packs per CYP assumes 35-pill packs (Data for Impact (D4I), n.d.; Lebetkin et al. 2024). Using 12 cycles per CYP for combined pills overstates their protection by 25%.

WarningThese weights are illustrative

The schedules in Table 3 are working assumptions. Replace them with continuation rates from local data, such as DHS contraceptive calendar data, before using the model for inference.

For the IUD, years 1 and 2 are set directly and the remaining balance \(R = 4.6 - 0.85 - 0.75 = 3.0\) is spread over years 3–10 on a linear decline. For \(n = 8\) years in an arithmetic sequence with first value \(a\) and last value \(b\), the sum is \(n(a + b)/2\), so \(a + b = 2R/n = 0.75\). We set \(a = 0.65\), continuing the 0.10 step between years 1 and 2, which gives \(b = 0.10\) and a step of \(-0.55/7\).

Pills have \(L_m = 1\), so \(P_{m,t} = c_m D_{m,t}\) and the two approaches coincide unless calendar alignment (Equation 8) is applied.

6 Worked example

Suppose 100, 120, 90 and 110 implants are inserted in 2021–2024, with no insertions before 2021 or after 2024. Applying Equation 2:

\[ \begin{aligned} P_{2021} &= 1.0 \, D_{2021} \\ P_{2022} &= 1.0 \, D_{2022} + 0.8 \, D_{2021} \\ P_{2023} &= 1.0 \, D_{2023} + 0.8 \, D_{2022} + 0.7 \, D_{2021} \\ P_{2024} &= 1.0 \, D_{2024} + 0.8 \, D_{2023} + 0.7 \, D_{2022} \end{aligned} \]

Both columns total 1,050 CYP. Standard reporting places all of it in 2021–2024; the weighted allocation carries 228 CYP into 2025–2026.

7 Start of the series

Equation 2 needs distribution data for \(L_m - 1\) years before the first estimation year. Without them, early years omit protection from earlier cohorts and are biased downward. For a 10-year IUD this needs nine prior years of insertion data. Where these are unavailable, either begin estimation \(L_m - 1\) years after the data start, or back-cast earlier distribution and report the assumption.

8 Calendar-year alignment

The weights refer to years since insertion, but insertions occur throughout the calendar year. If insertions are spread evenly over year \(s\), each year of use straddles two calendar years. The calendar-year weights are:

\[ \tilde{w}_{m,k} = \begin{cases} \tfrac{1}{2} w_{m,0} & k = 0 \\ \tfrac{1}{2} \left( w_{m,k-1} + w_{m,k} \right) & 1 \le k \le L_m - 1 \\ \tfrac{1}{2} w_{m,L_m-1} & k = L_m \end{cases} \tag{8}\]

This extends the window to \(L_m + 1\) calendar years while preserving Equation 4. To use it, replace \(w_{m,k}\) with \(\tilde{w}_{m,k}\) in Equation 2 and sum to \(k = L_m\). For the implant, \(\tilde{w} = (0.50, 0.90, 0.75, 0.35)\). For pills, half of each year’s protection moves to the following year.

9 Link to births

A birth in calendar year \(t+1\) was conceived roughly between April of year \(t\) and March of year \(t+1\). With conceptions spread evenly, births averted in \(t+1\) are:

\[ \hat{A}_{t+1} = \beta \left[ \varphi \, P_t + (1 - \varphi) \, P_{t+1} \right], \qquad \varphi = 0.75 \tag{9}\]

where \(\beta\) is births averted per CYP. \(\beta\) depends on fecundity and on what the users would have done without the method, and must be sourced separately.

10 Sensitivity analysis

Report results under at least:

  1. Standard CYP (Equation 1), as the reference.
  2. Even allocation, \(w_{m,k} = c_m / L_m\).
  3. The continuation schedule in Table 3.
  4. With and without calendar alignment (Equation 8).
