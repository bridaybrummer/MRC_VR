1 Overview

Report generated: 2026-08-18 14:05:17 SAST  ·  Timestamp shown for auditability — re-rendering this dashboard refreshes it against the latest source data.

This dashboard compares vital registration (VR) data with alternative mortality data sources for monitoring and evaluation purposes:

  • Tembisa Model: Demographic projections for HIV/AIDS and TB mortality
  • NCODV Study: Verbal autopsy-corrected estimates (2017-2018)
  • NMC Database: Notifiable Medical Conditions surveillance data for TB and poisoning

3.2 Timeliness vs. Late-Transfer Completeness

Each death record carries a date of death and a date of registration. The gap between them — the registration lag — drives a fundamental tradeoff: publishing statistics soon after the death year is timely but misses deaths transferred/registered late, while waiting longer captures those late transfers and is more complete. Using registration timing as a proxy, the curve below shows, for each death-year cohort, the cumulative share of its registered deaths captured by N years after death.

The 2022 death cohort is not yet complete: late transfers/registrations for 2022 deaths are still arriving, so its curve (like 2021’s) is right-truncated and will keep rising slightly with future extracts.

NoteInterpreting the Tradeoff
  • The height at lag 0 is the timely share — deaths registered in the same calendar year (≈ 98.5–99.2% across cohorts), so South African VR registration is highly timely.
  • The rise between lag 0 and lag 1 is the completeness gain from late transfers captured by waiting one extra year; it adds only ≈ 0.6–1.3 percentage points, after which the curves plateau.
  • Tradeoff implication: publishing one year after the death year (dashed line) captures ≈ 99.8% of all eventually-registered deaths. This 1-year cut-off is stricter than the WHO-recommended 2-year interval for CRVS completeness assessment, so the completeness penalty for timely release is small.
  • Recent cohorts (2021–2022) are right-truncated — they can only be observed to registration year 2024 — so their curves stop at a shorter lag, and 2022 is not yet a complete cohort.
Warning⚠ Data limitations & checks to investigate

These handling decisions are applied above so the plot is honest, but each one flags a record-level issue that should be checked at source rather than silently corrected:

  • 2013 is a partial cohort (3,226 records) and is excluded from cohort comparisons — its same-year registration share (≈ 54%) reflects a truncated extract window, not poor timeliness. Check: confirm the extract’s start date and whether earlier 2013 deaths live in a separate file.
  • Negative registration lags (1 record(s)) imply a death registered before it occurred — logically impossible. These are floored to lag 0 for plotting. Check: audit these records for transposed or mis-keyed death/registration dates.
  • Very late registrations (>5 years: 427 records) are capped at lag 5 in the cumulative curve. Check: verify whether these are genuine late registrations (e.g. delayed/contested deaths) or data-entry errors in the year fields.

None of these materially change the headline (≈ 99.8% within one year), but they should be triaged as part of routine VR data-quality control.

5 Data Quality: Ill-Defined vs. Garbage/Unusable Codes

Confirmed definitions — garbage/ill-defined/unusable categories below match the 5-category Global Burden of Disease (Naghavi et al. 2010) unusable-code crosswalk as applied to South African CRVS data by the SAMRC’s rapid assessment (4). Full definitions, ICD-10 code lists, and international-standard caveats are in Methods: Ill-Defined and Garbage/Unusable Cause-of-Death Definitions.

  • Garbage = Unusable (used interchangeably in this section).
  • Ill-defined (natural) = ICD-10 R00–R99 symptoms/signs, confirmed here to match garbage_flag_label == "Symptoms, signs and ill-defined conditions" exactly (648,981 deaths, last 10 years). Nannan et al. (2023) report this category nationally at ~13% of deaths (2000-2017), above their cited ideal level of below 5%.
  • Garbage/unusable is understood as ill-defined natural (R00–R99) + impossible + intermediate + immediate + insufficiently specified causes (the last of which includes X59, exposure to unspecified factor). Nannan et al. (2023) report ~32% of SA deaths nationally coded to unusable causes overall (2000-2017).
Note

7 HIV/AIDS Deaths

7.1 HIV/AIDS Mortality: VR vs Models

NoteKey Findings
  • VR data substantially underestimates HIV/AIDS mortality compared to the Tembisa model
  • The NCODV study suggests ~23% of deaths may be HIV-related (2017-2018)
  • The gap has narrowed over time but remains significant
ImportantSo what? — Decision this data informs

Monitored action: correct certification and coding of HIV/AIDS as the underlying cause of death.

The gap between VR and the Tembisa model is driven mainly by HIV deaths being mis-certified to indicator or “garbage” causes (e.g. TB, pneumonia, anaemia, wasting) rather than to HIV/AIDS — a recognised driver of cause-of-death undercount in high-prevalence settings (1,3).

  • Trigger: a persistent VR-vs-model gap, and an NCODV reattribution share (~23% of deaths HIV-related, 2017–2018) far exceeding the HIV fraction recorded in VR.
  • Decision: prioritise MCCD certifier training on naming HIV/AIDS as the underlying cause, and apply NCODV-based reattribution before reporting HIV mortality from VR.
  • Expected effect: the VR series moves toward the Tembisa model line as certification improves.
  • Monitor: re-render this dashboard each cycle (see the Report generated timestamp) and track narrowing of the VR-vs-model gap.

8 TB Deaths

8.1 TB Mortality: VR vs Models

ImportantSo what? — Decision this data informs

Monitored action: correct certification and coding of TB as the underlying cause of death.

TB mortality in VR diverges from the Tembisa model and NMC notifications largely because TB deaths are mis-certified to terminal or ill-defined causes, or lost where HIV/TB co-morbidity is not correctly sequenced on the MCCD (1,3).

  • Trigger: a VR count that sits below the Tembisa model and/or NMC notification trend for the same period.
  • Decision: prioritise MCCD certifier training on correct underlying-cause selection for TB (including HIV/TB sequencing), and reconcile VR against NMC notifications.
  • Expected effect: the VR series converges toward the model and notification trends as certification and coding improve.
  • Monitor: re-render this dashboard each cycle (see the Report generated timestamp) and track convergence of the VR, model, and notification series.

12 Methods

12.1 Garbage Code Classification

Deaths are classified using the WHO garbage code framework. A death is considered “not garbage” if the underlying cause code provides meaningful epidemiological information.

12.2 Ill-Defined and Garbage/Unusable Cause-of-Death Definitions

Status: confirmed. The definitions below match the 5-category unusable-code crosswalk (based on Naghavi et al. 2010’s GBD classification) as applied to South African CRVS data by the SAMRC’s rapid assessment of cause-of-death data (4). The garbage_flag_label categories already present in this dataset’s source pipeline correspond exactly to Nannan et al. (2023)’s five categories.

  • Ill-defined (natural) causes: ICD-10 Chapter XVIII, R00–R99 (“Symptoms, signs and abnormal clinical/laboratory findings, not elsewhere classified”). In this dataset this corresponds exactly to garbage_flag_label == "Symptoms, signs and ill-defined conditions" (verified 1:1 against a direct R00-R99 regex on UnderlyingCause). Nannan et al. (2023) report this nationally at ~13% of deaths (2000-2017), above their cited ideal level of below 5%.
  • Garbage / unusable codes (used interchangeably here): the broader GBD “unusable code” set — causes too vague, intermediate, or implausible to be the true underlying cause of death. Operationally this dashboard treats any garbage_flag > 0 category as garbage/unusable, composed of the five categories confirmed in Nannan et al. (2023), Table 6:
    1. Ill-defined natural causes (R00–R99, above);
    2. Impossible causes of death (e.g. essential primary hypertension, atherosclerosis, long-term sequelae — implausible as an underlying cause);
    3. Intermediate causes of death (e.g. heart failure, septicaemia, peritonitis, osteomyelitis, pulmonary embolism — a consequence of another condition, not the root cause);
    4. Immediate causes of death (e.g. cardiac arrest, respiratory failure — a terminal mode of dying, not a specific disease);
    5. Insufficiently specified causes within ICD chapters (not-otherwise-specified codes, e.g. unspecified cancer site).
    Nannan et al. (2023) report ~32% of SA deaths nationally coded to unusable causes overall (2000-2017), with ill-defined natural causes the largest single contributor (12-14%).
  • X59 (“Exposure to unspecified factor causing other and unspecified injury”) falls within Category 5 (Insufficiently specified) per the GBD Appendix 5 code list (alongside Y10-Y34), not as its own standalone garbage_flag category in the source pipeline — it is monitored separately here (see Data Quality: Ill-Defined vs. Garbage/Unusable Codes) because of its specific relevance to injury/undetermined-intent deaths.

Caveats vs. international (WHO/GBD) standards:

  • The WHO/GBD garbage-code framework additionally reclassifies some causes by age and sex (e.g. certain cardiovascular or cancer codes are only “garbage” in specific age groups) (3). The current garbage_flag in this dataset is not age/sex-stratified, so the % garbage reported here is not guaranteed to be directly comparable to published GBD “garbage-code” percentages for other countries — treat cross-study comparisons as indicative, not exact.
  • The 80%/20% usability thresholds displayed on these charts are the Mahapatra (2007) CRVS-series convention (2), consistent with the wider WHO/GBD literature (1,3), and are a general usability benchmark, not a South Africa-specific target — Nannan et al. (2023) instead cite a stricter below 5% ideal specifically for ill-defined natural causes (R00-R99) in the SA context, separate from the overall garbage/unusable total.
  • “Not-specified” facility/institution (DeathInst == "Unspecified") is a data-collection quality issue, distinct from ill-defined cause coding — the two are reported separately in this dashboard and should not be conflated when interpreting “garbage” percentages.
